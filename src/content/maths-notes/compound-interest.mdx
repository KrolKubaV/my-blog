The **Rule of 72** is a quick way to estimate how long it takes for an investment to double at a given annual interest rate.

## The Formula

If $r$ is the annual interest rate (as a percentage), the doubling time $T$ in years is approximately:

$$T \approx \frac{72}{r}$$

## Derivation

Starting with the compound interest formula:

$$A = P(1 + r)^t$$

For doubling, we need $A = 2P$, so:

$$2P = P(1 + r)^t$$

$$2 = (1 + r)^t$$

Taking logarithms:

$$t = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1 + r)}$$

For small $r$, we can approximate $\ln(1 + r) \approx r$, giving:

$$t \approx \frac{\ln 2}{r} \approx \frac{0.693}{r}$$

The Rule of 72 uses $0.72$ instead of $0.693$ for easier mental arithmetic.

## Example

At 8% annual interest:

$$T \approx \frac{72}{8} = 9 \text{ years}$$

The exact answer is $\frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.08} \approx 9.006$ years.
