The **Kelly Criterion** determines the optimal fraction of wealth to bet on a favorable gamble to maximize long-term growth rate.

<Definition title="Setup">
Consider a gamble with probability $p$ of winning and probability $q = 1 - p$ of losing. If you win, you receive $b$ dollars for every dollar bet.
</Definition>

<Theorem title="Kelly Fraction">
The optimal fraction of wealth to bet is:

$$f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}$$
</Theorem>

## Derivation

The expected logarithmic growth rate is:

$$G(f) = p \ln(1 + bf) + q \ln(1 - f)$$

Taking the derivative and setting it to zero:

$$G'(f) = \frac{pb}{1 + bf} - \frac{q}{1 - f} = 0$$

Solving for $f$:

$$f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}$$

<Remark>
For blackjack with $p = 0.6$, $b = 1$, the optimal bet is 20% of your bankroll: $f^* = 0.2$.
</Remark>
