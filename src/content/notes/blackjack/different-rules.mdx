Not all blackjack tables are the same. Casinos tweak the rules, and each tweak
shifts the odds — sometimes by a little, sometimes enough to turn a good game
into a bad one.

## The main rule variations

- **Number of decks** — single-deck games deal a natural slightly more often;
  eight-deck shoes dilute the effect of removal.
- **Dealer hits or stands on soft 17 (H17/S17)** — H17 costs the player roughly
  $0.2\%$ in expected value, because the dealer gets a second chance to improve
  a hand that cannot bust.
- **Double after split allowed (DAS)** — worth about $0.14\%$ to the player,
  since some pairs become much more profitable when doubling is available.
- **Blackjack payout** — the big one. A natural paying $6{:}5$ instead of
  $3{:}2$ costs around $1.4\%$, more than almost any other single rule.

## Why strategy depends on the rules

Basic strategy is not one table but a family of tables. Removing the option to
double after splitting changes what is optimal for a pair of $8$s; a dealer who
hits soft 17 makes standing on soft 18 against a $6$ correct instead of
marginal. Whenever you sit down, check the placard listing the rules first —
the cheapest seats are rarely the best games.
