The **Kelly Criterion** determines the optimal fraction of wealth to bet on a favorable gamble to maximize long-term growth rate.

## Setup

Suppose you have a bet with:
- Probability of winning $p$
- Odds of $b:1$ (you win $b$ for every $1$ wagered)

The optimal fraction of wealth to bet is:

$$f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}$$

where $q = 1 - p$.

## Derivation

If you bet fraction $f$ of your wealth, your wealth after one bet is:

$$W = \begin{cases} W_0(1 + bf) & \text{with probability } p \\ W_0(1 - f) & \text{with probability } q \end{cases}$$

The expected logarithmic growth rate is:

$$G(f) = p \ln(1 + bf) + q \ln(1 - f)$$

Taking the derivative and setting it to zero:

$$G'(f) = \frac{pb}{1 + bf} - \frac{q}{1 - f} = 0$$

Solving:

$$f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}$$

## Example

A coin flip game where you win \$2 for heads and lose \$1 for tails, with $p = 0.6$:

$$f^* = \frac{2 \cdot 0.6 - 0.4}{2} = \frac{1.2 - 0.4}{2} = 0.4$$

You should bet 40% of your wealth each time.

```python
import math

def kelly_fraction(p, b):
    """Optimal fraction to bet given win probability p and odds b."""
    q = 1 - p
    return (b * p - q) / b

# Example: 60% win probability, 2:1 odds
f = kelly_fraction(0.6, 2)
print(f"Optimal bet fraction: {f:.1%}")  # 40.0%
```
