<Problem>

On an island, $100$ people have blue eyes and the rest have brown. Everyone can see everyone else's eye colour but not their own. No one may communicate about eye colour. If a person discovers they have blue eyes, they must leave the island at midnight.

A visitor announces: "At least one of you has blue eyes."

What happens?

</Problem>

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

All $100$ blue-eyed people leave on the $100$th night.

With $n$ blue-eyed people:

- If $n = 1$: that person sees no other blue eyes, knows they must be blue, leaves on night $1$.
- If $n = 2$: each sees one blue eye. When that person doesn't leave on night $1$, they deduce they must also have blue eyes. Both leave on night $2$.
- By induction: all $n$ leave on night $n$.

The visitor's statement seems to add no information, but it establishes **common knowledge** — everyone knows that everyone knows that... everyone knows at least one person has blue eyes. This is the key to the chain of deductions.

</details>
